Embeddings show a clear signal on known benchmarks. On the six unseen benchmarks the two MLP probes pass the criterion we fixed in advance, with pooled Spearman $0.090$ and $0.103$ against $0.026$ for a random baseline and a positive correlation on 5 of 6 benchmarks; the ridge probes do not ($0.065$ and $0.047$). On the three test benchmarks alone the margin over the random baseline is small ($0.109$ and $0.104$ against $0.076$) and rests on three benchmarks.

This small signal does not help prediction. Table~\ref{tab:probe-prior} shows that no scale $\gamma$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:probe-prior} helps: every $\gamma\ge0.25$ is worse than $\gamma=0$ on both splits, and the best scale chosen on validation ($\gamma=0.05$, Rasch-target MLP) is within $0.0004$ of $\gamma=0$ on both. This tests these two probes only; a stronger difficulty predictor, for instance one trained through an item response likelihood on more items, could do better.
